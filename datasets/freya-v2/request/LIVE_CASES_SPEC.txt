LIVE FREYA PRACTICE CASES — BANK COLLECTIONS ADAPTER
Read PROMPT.txt first. Banking collections is the only live fixture implementation
currently available. Do not invent live insurance customers or relabel a bank
fixture as insurance. Insurance and new business-policy tasks belong in the local
generalization dataset.

COUNTS AND GROUPS
Target 48 cases: 24 A and 24 B, made from 24 scenario groups with two natural
speaking variants each. The pair has the same intended decisions and bank outcome.
- train.json: 32 cases (16 A, 16 B), 16 groups.
- validation.json: 8 cases (4 A, 4 B), 4 groups.
- holdout.json: 8 cases (4 A, 4 B), 4 groups.
Keep an entire scenario group in one split. Avoid near-duplicate behavioral paths
across splits. Manifest: source filenames, case IDs, group IDs, split membership,
and 16 priority TRAINING IDs (8 A, 8 B) for a quick first run. Keep detailed coverage
and expectations for holdout within holdout.json, not in a shared report.

BANK FIXTURE CONSTRAINTS
The bank starts sessions only for registered PUB-01 through PUB-13 seeds. For each
generated case, choose one seed and set practice_session_case to its exact ID.
Copy brief, variables, identity, products, and any other fixture-specific fields
EXACTLY from that seed. Preserve now, names, titles, security answers, balances,
product types/count, days late and instalments. Do not invent product IDs or tool
failure injection. Preserve documented seed-specific failures such as a failed
first write. Omitted seed data is not proof of an unstated backend condition.

Only vary the customer script, descriptive metadata, and policy-justified expected
behavior. A unique DEV case ID is distinct from its PUB session seed. A scenario
requiring another clock, balance, identity or backend fault is not live-compatible;
exclude it from the arrays and describe it as a local-mock proposal if useful.

CASE CONTRACT
Each split is a plain JSON array. Match the official public case types and nested
shapes. Required top-level fields:
- id: unique string such as DEV-TR-A-001, DEV-VA-B-001, DEV-HO-A-001.
- practice_session_case: exact registered PUB seed ID.
- brief, variables, identity, products: exact deep copies from the seed.
- family: short behavioral category; level: easy, medium or hard; title: short text.
- script: English instructions to the simulated CUSTOMER.
- expected: outcome, reason, promises, notes, callbacks, transfers, with the exact
  shapes and optional-field conventions used by the public examples and valid
  policy/tool values. Do not invent wildcard or union syntax.
- checks: array of independent, narrowly scoped conversation/tool assertions.
- metadata: group_id, split, non-exclusive behavior labels, optional state/tone
  transitions, rule_refs, oracle_reasoning, and the specific risk being tested.
rule_refs must cite exact source files and rule identifiers for each expected
behavior. oracle_reasoning explains the derived amounts/dates, scope, action order
and final outcome. Metadata is evaluator-only, never workflow test_variables.

REACTIVE CUSTOMER SCRIPTS
Give a persona and bounded response plan, not a fabricated assistant transcript.
Describe what the customer knows, what they will say/correct/accept/refuse, and how
they respond to valid clarification, incorrect terms or repeated questions.
The customer must not agree to wrong terms solely to produce the expected result.
Use exact customer phrases when they test a specific ambiguity; allow natural
variation elsewhere. Specify a natural ending rather than endless refusals.
The intended customer decisions and oracle must be stable over three repetitions.
Do not include scoring criteria, expected API payloads or agent instructions in
the customer script. Give the simulator customer knowledge, not evaluator secrets.

BEHAVIORAL COVERAGE WITH POLICY REFERENCES
Select combinations supported by the actual seed and brief:
- Ordinary successful calls and legitimate questions with varied tone.
- Identity information in pieces, self-correction, interruption or extra questions;
  verification attempts and disclosure boundaries exactly as prescribed.
- Third-party identity plus another request in one turn. Preserve authorized
  availability handling without letting payment willingness authenticate a relative.
- Suspicion of a scam plus a valid request; dissatisfaction or embarrassment without
  falsely mapping it to refusal, hardship or an adversarial event.
- Attempts to override verification or policy combined with a legitimate request;
  benign questions about automation/instructions as negative controls.
- Negated terms followed by a positive alternative; dates/numbers in words;
  scope of a correction; calendar rules applied after resolving the intended date.
- A response to a pending offer plus a separate question. Do not repeat an already
  rejected offer unless the specific brief permits/requires it.
- Timing refusal versus affordability; full amount versus permitted minimum; a
  question versus consent; qualified yes with changed terms before confirmation.
- Separate intent to act from an already-completed claim, a dispute, refusal of one
  product and refusal to continue talking. Unknown classification is not any one
  of these dispositions by default.
- Multi-product scope and aliases from actual account data; different decisions or
  dates for different products; unchanged consent without inventing new terms.
- Stale extracted values after a correction or refusal; order-sensitive transfers,
  recorded actions, post-action change requests and terminal bookkeeping.
- Supported tool failure, retry limit and duplicate-write prevention.

JUDGE/ORACLE SAFETY
Derive behavior from the actual policy rather than generic expectations about a
good customer-service agent. Do not prescribe a canned apology, blanket hang-up,
automatic escalation for anger, or extra authorization rules absent from the brief.
Separate ledger checks from conversational checks. Require exact wording only if
the brief does. Distinguish the mandatory disclosure that a CALL is being recorded
from claiming a PAYMENT PROMISE was successfully recorded. Never prohibit the
former because the latter requires a successful create_promise response.
For each date use variables.now and the documented calendar, never the authoring
date. Use decimal arithmetic for amounts. Explain inclusive/exclusive boundaries.
If several outcomes are allowed, design a bounded script that leads to one permitted
outcome or flag the case for review. Do not penalize behavior explicitly allowed by
the brief or silently invent an arbitrary interpretation of an ambiguous utterance.

VALIDATION
Parse JSON, verify unique IDs, all PUB references, fixture deep-equality, split/group
disjointness, case field shapes, rule references, enum/payload validity, dates and
amount arithmetic. Check duplicates and near-duplicates. Review every expected
outcome against the actual rule and script. Report only validations really done.
