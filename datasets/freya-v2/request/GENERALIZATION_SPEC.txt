NEW BRIEF GENERALIZATION — LOCAL MOCK DATA ONLY
Read PROMPT.txt first. This tests whether the builder can learn a different set of
rules from its INPUT. It must not learn a fixed collections policy from the dataset.

TARGET
Create 6 original synthetic brief bundles: 3 banking and 3 insurance.
- train/: 1 banking and 1 insurance bundle.
- validation/: 1 banking and 1 insurance bundle.
- holdout/: 1 banking and 1 insurance bundle.
Give each bundle 4 reactive customer cases, 24 cases total. Include at least one
ordinary successful case, one mixed-intent case, one information/correction/scope
case and one policy-boundary or supported-tool-failure case. They can have diverse
and changing emotions without emotion determining the expected business outcome.

Keep different business-policy families in different splits; a renamed policy or
different threshold on an otherwise copied brief is not an independent family.
Holdout policy content, answers and scripts stay exclusively under holdout/.
All variants or paired policy counterfactuals from the same family stay together.

BUSINESS SCOPE
Use varied banking and insurance tasks. Possible examples are banking servicing,
collections or account-security requests; insurance renewal, claim intake or policy
servicing. These are suggestions, not quotas or statements about hidden test topics.
Pick tasks you can define completely and test deterministically. Do not spread into
unrelated sectors or make unsupported claims about real regulations or products.
All bank/insurer rules in this section are invented SYNTHETIC test policy, not legal
advice and not official Freya policy. Label them accordingly.

FORMAT VARIATION
Across the 6 bundles provide both clean specifications and raw document bundles.
At least 3 bundles should combine an email, a policy excerpt, an amendment/memo and
a code list. The correct order/scope of amendments must be explicitly resolvable
from those documents. Do not create an unsolvable contradiction and guess an oracle.

Vary the data needed to interpret the brief: identity requirements, authorization
boundaries, permitted disclosures, thresholds/deadlines, calendars, confirmation
conditions, retries, action order, per-entity state and closure codes, where relevant.
Some tasks may not have a minimum offer, holiday adjustment, payment promise, callback
or identity challenge. Their absence matters: the builder must not import those
rules merely because they existed in the public collections example.

TOOL CONTRACT BOUNDARY
For a bank collection variant intended to resemble the documented final contract,
retain the official seven tool names while making any changed fields/enums explicit
in the synthetic OpenAPI. For other banking/insurance tasks, a self-contained MOCK
API may use task-appropriate tools. Flag that as an extended LOCAL contract, not as
an officially supported Freya final tool contract. Do not rename create_promise to
mean approval of an insurance claim or otherwise hide a semantic mismatch.
Use reserved .invalid URLs, such as https://mock-bank.invalid or
https://mock-insurance.invalid. No real endpoints, credentials or real customer data.
Never attach practice_session_case to a generalization case or point it at the live
bank endpoint. We must implement its fake tools before it can run.

BUNDLE FILES
generalization/<split>/<bundle-id>/
  manifest.json
  brief.txt OR documents/email.txt, policy.txt, amendment.txt, codes.json
  openapi.json
  mock-fixture.json
  cases.json
  oracle.json

manifest.json:
  id, brief_family_id, split, sector (banking or insurance),
  policy_origin: synthetic,
  execution_target: local_mock_only,
  tool_contract: official_names_variant or extended_local_contract,
  source_files: exact relative paths used as BUILDER INPUT,
  evaluator_files: fixture/cases/oracle paths kept OUT of builder input,
  scope_notes and assumptions.

openapi.json:
  Valid OpenAPI 3.x with operationIds, required/optional request fields, response
  envelopes, enums and failure responses needed by the cases. Specify operation
  semantics in descriptions and their required policy references. Avoid unused APIs.

mock-fixture.json:
  Synthetic account/policy/request entities, initial state, supplied clock/timezone,
  identifier mapping, operation preconditions, deterministic state transitions and
  any explicit scripted failure followed by success. For each case define a fresh
  independent initial state; cases must not depend on another case running first.
  Define repeated-call behavior so duplicate irreversible actions are detectable.
  This is a mock SPECIFICATION; do not claim it is executable without an adapter.

cases.json:
  A plain array of 4 objects containing id, group_id, split, variables,
  customer_private_facts, script, behavior metadata and fixture_reference.
  Customer scripts follow the same reactive, bounded, non-leaking rules as the live
  dataset. They should test the supplied policy, not teach it to the agent.

oracle.json:
  Keyed by case ID, with policy rule references, rationale, required/forbidden
  disclosures/actions, payload expectations, ordering constraints, call count bounds,
  final state/outcome and narrowly scoped conversation assertions.
  Do not force one arbitrary tool sequence when several sequences meet the policy;
  express partial-order constraints or clearly separate permitted alternatives.
  Define explicit success conditions and distinguish irreversible effects from
  utterances promising an effect. Expected values must derive from the brief and
  fixture, not from unstated banking/insurance convention.

Builder-visible source documents and OpenAPI describe the POLICY. Do not include
the test customer's private answers, expected transcript or oracle there.
Evaluation artifacts are separate and must never become hidden hints in the brief.

VALIDATION
Parse all JSON and validate OpenAPI structure when tooling is available. Verify
all rule/document/fixture references; request and response examples against their
schemas; enum consistency; clock/calendar and amount arithmetic; amendment scope;
case independence; and satisfiability of every expected final state.
Review whether a correct agent can actually achieve each oracle using only the
declared mock operations. No missing action or fabricated state transition.
Report tooling used, real checks performed and remaining ambiguities. Do not claim
the builder or mock environment has been executed.

generalization/manifest.json may list bundle IDs, sectors, splits, paths and counts.
Keep holdout-specific behavior summaries, unique policy values and review notes
inside the holdout directory. Do not expose them in the shared training report.
